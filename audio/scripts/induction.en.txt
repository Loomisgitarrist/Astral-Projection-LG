# SEGMENT 1 — ARRIVAL (target: 2:00, ~230 words)
# =====================================================================
Find a quiet room. Lie on your back. Palms facing up. Let your feet fall slightly apart. Allow your jaw to drop a fraction of an inch. Head on a thin pillow, or none at all.

Close your eyes, but don't try to clear your mind yet. Let the thoughts arrive as they will. We're going to soften every part of you tonight — starting at the top of your head, and working all the way down to your toes. There's nothing you have to do, and nowhere you have to be.

Before we begin, set one intention. Silently — just inside your own head — say to yourself: I will consciously leave my body tonight. Say it once, with quiet conviction, and then let it go. Don't repeat it. The moment of declaration is the seed. From this point on, your only job is to relax.

# =====================================================================
# SEGMENT 2 — PROGRESSIVE RELAXATION HEAD TO TOES (target: 4:30, ~520 words)
# =====================================================================
Now we'll begin to soften the body. We'll move from the top of your head, down to your toes. There's no rush. If you fall asleep during this part, that's fine. The body has to fall asleep before the mind can stay awake. Sleep is welcome here.

Begin with the scalp. Imagine the skin across the top of your head loosening — like ice that's been sitting in warm water for a few minutes. The forehead follows. The small muscles around your eyes. The muscles between your eyebrows. The bridge of your nose, your cheeks, the muscles of your ears.

Allow your jaw to drop a fraction more. The tongue falls back toward the throat. The lips part just slightly. The muscles inside your mouth soften.

Move down to your neck. The long muscles on either side of the throat. The base of the skull. The place where your neck meets your shoulders. You might notice tension here. Don't fight it — just breathe into it, and on the next exhale, let it go.

The shoulders are next. Most of us hold enormous tension here, all day, every day. Imagine the shoulder muscles softening — letting the arms feel heavier than the rest of the body. The upper arms. The elbows. The forearms. The wrists.

Now the hands. Each finger, one at a time, beginning to soften. The palms. The backs of the hands. The wrists again. Imagine your hands are heavy and warm — maybe even slightly tingling, in a pleasant way.

Move to the chest. The rib muscles. The intercostals between the ribs. Imagine the breath moving through these muscles without effort, without resistance. The heart is beating, but you don't have to think about it. The lungs are breathing, but you don't have to think about them.

The belly. The deep muscles of the abdomen. The lower back. The hips. The pelvis. Imagine the pelvis softening into the mattress — your whole lower body releasing its weight downward, into the bed.

The thighs. Both thighs at once. The kneecaps. The shins. The calves. The ankles. And finally the feet — the arches, the balls of the feet, each toe. The whole body now is heavy. The whole body now is warm. The whole body now is letting go.

# =====================================================================
# SEGMENT 3 — BREATH AND FOCUS 10 (target: 2:30, ~290 words)
# =====================================================================
Now we'll slow the breath. Begin to breathe a little more deeply than you've been breathing. Long, slow exhales. Inhale through the nose for about four counts. Exhale through the mouth — or the nose — for six, or seven, or eight counts. There's no need to count exactly. Just make the exhale longer than the inhale.

Within a minute or two you'll begin to feel heavy. Warmer. More deeply relaxed than you were a moment ago. This is what we're aiming for. Stay with the breath.

A breath happens. Then the next breath happens. Then the next. You might notice, at some point, that you can't quite remember whether you've breathed in the last thirty seconds. When that happens, you're at what Robert Monroe called Focus Ten — a state where the body is deeply asleep, and the mind remains conscious and awake. We're going to settle into this state for a few minutes.

If your mind wanders, that's fine. Just notice it. Don't follow the thought. Let it pass through you like a cloud passing through a still sky. And come back to the breath.

You might notice images appearing behind your closed eyes. Colors. Faces. Brief scenes. These are hypnagogic images, and they're a sign that you're in the right place. You don't have to do anything with them. Watch them come and go.

# =====================================================================
# SEGMENT 4 — VIBRATIONAL STATE AND EXIT (target: 3:30, ~400 words)
# =====================================================================
We're going to wait, now, for what Monroe called the vibrational state. This is a band of sensations that many practitioners notice just before an exit. Not everyone notices it. If you don't notice it tonight, that's fine — we'll work with what does appear.

The vibrational state, when it comes, can feel like this. A high-pitched tone in the ears. A buzzing or vibrating sensation that runs through the body, or just outside it. A rushing white-noise sound. A feeling of pressure, often at the forehead or the chest. Mental images that begin to take on life — they move, they have detail, they persist.

If you notice any of these sensations, do not try to make them stop. Just stay calm. Stay with them. They are the doorway, and they're opening now.

When you're ready — or when the impulse arrives — you'll choose one of four exits. You don't have to think about which one. Your body will know.

The lift-out. Imagine your awareness gently rising straight up out of your chest, like a helium balloon. Don't yank — just imagine the upward motion and let it continue.

The roll-out. Imagine your body rolling to one side, off the bed, just as you might roll over in sleep. The first few seconds will feel as though the physical body really is rolling. Then you'll find yourself somewhere else.

The rope. Imagine a glowing rope hanging above your chest. Reach up with one hand and pull it down a hand's width. Reach with the other. Repeat, until your awareness is sitting upright above the physical body.

The stand-up. The most direct. Simply intend: I am now standing up. Don't visualize it. Just assume the position. Your body will follow.

Choose whichever one fits the impulse. Trust the impulse. Don't second-guess.

# =====================================================================
# SEGMENT 5 — STABILIZE AND RETURN (target: 2:30, ~290 words)
# =====================================================================
The first five to ten seconds after exit are the most fragile. If you find yourself out of the body, move a hand. Wiggle the fingers of your astral hand, or the toes of your astral foot. Touch a wall. Touch the bed frame. Anything solid. This anchors you, and tells the non-physical body that you intend to stay here for a moment.

Speak. Out loud, if you can. Say: clarity now. Say: increase focus. Voice activates the non-physical body more reliably than anything else.

Look around. Describe the room aloud. Every detail you can see — for at least fifteen seconds. The more you describe, the more solid the scene becomes.

When you feel stable — and only when you feel stable — set an intention to return. To yourself. To your body. In a known number of minutes. The return is usually automatic. You'll feel a gentle tug — the silver cord idea, or simply the body reabsorbing attention.

If nothing happens tonight, that is the practice. The body knows what to do. Your only job is to show up tomorrow night, and the night after that.

Return whenever you're ready. The body will be here. It's been here the whole time.
