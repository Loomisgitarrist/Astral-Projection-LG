Method probabilities

Which method actually works?

Ranked by what the majority of people who have had an out-of-body experience report as working best — scored by the JEV probability engine across 12 real methods.

How to read the percentage. The number is an advisory probability, not a guarantee: it is the chance that a given method is the one most often credited as working best by real practitioners — based on community reports, surveys and documented success rates. The methods overlap, and nearly all of them share one core state: mind awake, body asleep.

Ranked by probability

1

Wake-Back-to-Bed (WBTB)

Sleep-based · Easy–moderate
57% probability

What it is. Sleep about 4–7 hours, wake briefly for 10–20 minutes, then return to bed with the single intention of staying aware as you fall back asleep.

Why it ranks here. After partial sleep your body is deeply relaxed while your mind is still close to dreaming — REM pressure is high, so the “mind awake, body asleep” threshold is the easiest to hold. This is the engine underneath most other methods, which is why it tops the ranking.

2

Indirect method · “The Phase” (Raduga)

Sleep-based · Easy
18% probability

What it is. On waking, don't move. Immediately run short “separation” attempts — imagine moving a hand, rolling out, standing up — in about one-minute cycles as you drift back toward sleep.

Why it ranks here. It captures the exact moment you are already in the right state instead of building it from scratch. Raduga's teaching data reports roughly 50% success within 1–3 days and 80% within 2–7 days — the fastest documented on-ramp, and the best pick for beginners.

3

Lucid-dream conversion (WILD)

Dream-based · Moderate
15% probability

What it is. Become aware inside a normal dream, then “leave” the dream body — spin, drop backwards through the floor, or fly up through the ceiling with the intent to project.

Why it ranks here. If you already dream lucidly you are already in a dissociated body-model, so stepping into an out-of-body state is a small shift rather than a big one. Practitioners call it the easiest route once lucid dreaming is established.

4

Sleep-paralysis transition

Sleep-based · Moderate
15% probability

What it is. When sleep paralysis happens on its own, stay calm instead of fighting it and gently amplify the floating or rising sensations.

Why it ranks here. Sleep paralysis already is the body-asleep / mind-awake state — the only missing pieces are calm and intent, so it can slide into an OBE quickly. It ranks high on success-when-it-happens, but it depends on the state occurring.

5

Deep relaxation + visualization (rope & roll-out)

Mind technique · Easy–moderate
14% probability

What it is. Relax completely, then vividly imagine separation — climbing a rope, rolling out to one side, or floating upward.

Why it ranks here. The classic method taught in most books. It works well for people with strong mental imagery, but it is slower because you have to reach the relaxed threshold starting from full waking consciousness.

6

Deep meditation / Yoga Nidra

Meditative · Easy–moderate
11% probability

What it is. Train calm, sustained awareness through body-scans, mindfulness or yoga nidra.

Why it ranks here. This is the foundation skill every other method depends on — many practitioners credit meditation, not a specific trick, for finally getting there. It ranks here only because it builds the state indirectly, over weeks.

7

Binaural beats / Hemi-Sync / Gateway Experience

Audio / frequency · Easy
10% probability

What it is. Listen to theta-range binaural audio (such as Monroe's Gateway tracks) through headphones.

Why it ranks here. It nudges the brain toward the hypnagogic theta state and gives you a routine to follow, so many people find it a genuine aid. It sits mid-table because most credit it as support beside another method, and the lab evidence is contested.

8

Affirmations + intention (Buhlman early-morning)

Mind technique · Easy
9% probability

What it is. Repeat out-of-body affirmations and set a strong, specific intention before sleep (Buhlman's nine-step “Early Morning Method”).

Why it ranks here. Cheap and easy, and intention is the “switch” many report flips the experience. On its own it usually needs a sleep transition to ride on, so it rarely works solo.

9

Direct method (mind-awake / body-asleep)

Meditative · Hard
9% probability

What it is. Stay fully conscious through the entire fall-asleep transition, never losing the thread of awareness.

Why it ranks here. Extremely powerful once mastered, but the hardest skill here: you must relax completely while refusing to fall asleep. Most people fail for a long time before it clicks.

10

Robert Bruce tactile imaging (New Energy Ways)

Energy / mind · Moderate
4% probability

What it is. Work with the “energy body” through touch-based sensations (brushing, sponging, rubbing) instead of mental pictures.

Why it ranks here. The go-to technique for people who can't visualise (aphantasia), and it produces strong sensations. It's specialised energy-work, though, so most practitioners never use it.

11

Hypnosis / self-hypnosis

Mind technique · Easy
3% probability

What it is. Use trance induction or guided self-hypnosis to sink into deep relaxation.

Why it ranks here. Reaches the trance threshold reliably for some people, but it's a minority technique in the community and rarely cited as the deciding factor.

12

Float tank / sensory deprivation

Environment · Easy
2% probability

What it is. Float in a tank (or otherwise strip away sight, sound and touch) to maximise relaxation and dissociation.

Why it ranks here. Deep sensory deprivation genuinely helps the dissociative state, but it needs special, often paid equipment — so very few use it and it is almost never the method that “does it”.
